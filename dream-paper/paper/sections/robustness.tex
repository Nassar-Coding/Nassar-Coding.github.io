\paragraph{Trend diagnostic and inference framing.} For every city,
regime, and pre-named contrast we regress the daily loss differential on
the day index and test the slope with a Newey--West (lag-28) statistic,
reporting also the share of the mean differential attributable to the
linear drift (trend-diagnostic table, supplement). The result is unambiguous: 45 of
48 differential series are trend-dominated (the only stationarity-
compatible cell is Chicago under generous capacity), and 11 of 12 loss
\emph{level} series trend as well. This is a structural property of the
carryover stock, strongest under scarcity but present broadly: policy
differences compound rather than fluctuate around a stationary mean.
Accordingly, all decision-contrast intervals in Section~\ref{sec:decision} are read as
descriptive characterizations of a systematic divergence within the
deterministic simulation; a stationary-inference reading is retained
only for the Chicago/generous cell. The forecast-accuracy contrasts of
Section~\ref{sec:forecast} are unaffected (their daily error differentials carry no
stock) and their inferential reading stands.

\paragraph{Block-length stability.} None of the 48 decision-contrast
interval conclusions changes when the bootstrap block length moves from
the primary 28 days to 14 or 56 days; the same holds for the forecast
contrasts. (Block-length stability alone cannot detect a common trend; the
trend diagnostic above is the binding check.)

\paragraph{Decision-layer sensitivity.} The pre-specified grid preserves
every qualitative conclusion: 10\% daily abandonment, one-family-at-a-time
service-yield multipliers of 0.70 and 1.30 over every active family, and
train+validation budget calibration each leave the policy ordering
unchanged in all cities, and the Austin analysis excluding the
heterogeneous \emph{other} family (budgets recomputed) reproduces the
primary ordering. Replacing the primary $b_0=0$ with a validation warm-up
carryover or each family's training-average daily demand leaves the
claim-bearing ordering (uniform worst; fixed-index point-greedy below the
three identified policies) unchanged in all 12 cells (supplement). Under
the normative-priority scenario the proportional--optimizer gap
compresses, which is why the policy-ordering claim is stated as
conditional on the equal-weight objective.

\paragraph{Allocation granularity ($\kappa$).}
A global sweep over $\kappa \in \{1,5,10,25,50,100\}$, budgets recomputed
from the same frozen training means, preserves the claim-bearing ordering
in all 72 city-regime-$\kappa$ cells: uniform worst, fixed-index
point-greedy below the identified policies. This includes $\kappa=1$, the
finest discrete request-equivalent granularity tested, where the
objective stays tie-dominated ($99.5\%$ mean tie share) and the
fixed-index penalty is largest, so the pathology is not an artifact of
coarse units; the proportional secondary rule tracks direct proportional
throughout ($\le 0.2\%$ below). Per-cell magnitudes are in the supplement.

\paragraph{Two-sided weather interpolation.}
Short temperature gaps are filled by two-sided interpolation
(Section~\ref{sec:data}), which can borrow a later value; dropping every
target whose weather predictors depend on an interpolated value (San
Francisco and Austin) and refitting moves test MAE by at most $1.3\%$ (San
Francisco; none in Austin), leaves the validation-selected model and all
feature-set and policy orderings unchanged, so the weather gain is not an
interpolation artifact.

\paragraph{What would change the conclusions.} The simulation results are
conditional on the stylization assumptions (interchangeable units, fixed
yield, no intra-day dynamics) and on the equal-weights objective; we
claim regime \emph{patterns} within this simulated environment, not
city-specific magnitudes. The forecasting results are unconditional on
those assumptions; the weather findings are conditional on day-ahead
forecast availability (A3), bounded by the lagged-weather variant.
