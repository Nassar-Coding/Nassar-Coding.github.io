## Corrected governing research question

> Across heterogeneous municipal 311 reporting systems, under pre-specified
> hypothetical service-pressure regimes, when do differences in next-day
> forecasts of reported administrative demand produce materially different
> outcomes in a transparent simulated family-level capacity allocation, and
> when are predictive rankings sufficient proxies for simulated decision
> rankings?

Paper identity: **benchmark-dominant** — forecasting is the strongest
validated evidence; the decision analysis is a bounded, explicitly simulated
evaluation over abstract request-equivalent capacity units.

## Pre-specified experimental grid (complete; nothing else is claimed)

- Capacity regimes: scarce/moderate/generous = 70% / 90% / 110% of
  training-window mean daily demand, kappa = 50 request-equivalents per unit.
- Objective: EQUAL family weights (primary); one normative priority scenario
  (water/safety 3, housing/streets/sanitation 2, others 1) as sensitivity,
  explicitly not socially validated.
- Service yield: homogeneous kappa (primary); one illustrative heterogeneous
  yield scenario (config-specified multipliers) as sensitivity, explicitly
  not observed productivity.
- Carryover: full carryover (primary); 10% abandonment sensitivity.
- Austin: repeat decision analysis with `other` excluded (sensitivity).
- Capacity calibration: training-only (primary); train+validation
  (sensitivity).
- Inference: paired moving-block bootstrap (block 28 d, 2000 resamples,
  seed 20260609) on daily loss differentials for the pre-named contrasts:
  (a) full-distribution vs median arm of the quantile model;
  (b) validation-selected best point model vs trailing-mean naive, greedy
      policy fixed;
  (c) greedy vs proportional, forecaster fixed;
  (d) decision-loss-selected vs MAE-selected model.
