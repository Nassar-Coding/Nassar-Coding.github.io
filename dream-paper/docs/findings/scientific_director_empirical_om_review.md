## What the paper actually is

This is a **reproducibility-first, four-city forecasting benchmark with an attached controlled simulation experiment about the forecast–policy interface**. Its real spine is: (i) a carefully provenanced multi-city daily 311 panel with frozen harmonization; (ii) a replication-and-heterogeneity study of public-signal value (calendar everywhere, weather in three of four cities, a retained Austin null); (iii) a temporally valid negative result on pooling and zero-shot transfer; and (iv) a simulation showing that *how* a forecast enters an allocation policy (degenerate point vs. full distribution; optimizer vs. proportional apportionment) can matter as much as which forecaster produced it, while accuracy-based and decision-based selection mostly coincide. The strongest results are about forecasts and their representation, not about operations: every operational quantity is simulated, every decision conclusion is conditional on a stylized environment, and the paper says so. The manuscript’s own title (“A Benchmark with a Controlled Simulated Capacity-Allocation Evaluation”) matches this identity — a rare case where the framing is *not* inflated.

**Dominant contribution classification: mixed, with one clearly dominant component — benchmark (empirical forecasting benchmark), supported by a decision-science layer.** It is not (yet) empirical operations management: no operational quantity is observed, and the decision findings characterize a simulation, not a service system. The manuscript does not claim otherwise; the only identity mismatch in the package is in the *older* docs (01_problem_definition’s “crews/backlog/priority-weighted” language), not in the rewritten paper.

## Strongest scientific contribution

The strongest defensible contribution is the **combination of a temporally valid multi-city replication of public-signal forecast value (including a clean null and clean negative-transfer results) with a controlled demonstration, inside one fitted model, that the representation of a forecast at the policy interface — degenerate point vs. full predictive distribution — changes simulated allocation outcomes in every tested setting, while model *rankings* by accuracy and by simulated decision loss largely coincide.** In one sentence: in this benchmark, the interface between forecast and policy matters more than marginal accuracy differences among good models, and the paper proves it under a protocol (frozen budgets, validation-only selection, stage censoring, guard suite) that a hostile reviewer can actually audit. The benchmark itself — versioned, regenerable, with the harmonization audit-then-freeze sequence on the record — is a reusable artifact independent of any conclusion.

## Strongest empirical findings (ranked)

1. **Calendar gains replicate in all four systems; weather value is heterogeneous with a retained Austin null.** *Evidence:* validation-selected test MAE drops 10.2–15.6% from internal to calendar in every city, CI-backed, stable across five rolling-origin folds; Austin weather effect +0.001 MAE, interval covering zero. *Importance:* converts a single-city finding (the author’s Paper 1 template, in the Cui/Steinker tradition) into a multi-system regularity plus a documented boundary. *Limitation:* weather value is conditional on assumption A3 (realized weather as a forecast proxy); the lagged-weather variant erases most of NYC’s gain — correctly disclosed. *Permitted claim:* exactly as written in §6.
1. **Temporally valid pooling and zero-shot transfer are mostly harmful here.** *Evidence:* post-C11 stage-censored pooled training raises test MAE significantly in three cities, neutral in one; censored zero-shot degrades +5% to +149%. *Importance:* a genuine, decision-relevant negative result against the “global models help” default (Salinas et al. tradition), with the contamination that previously distorted it detected and corrected on the record. *Limitation:* four Socrata cities, six years of local history each — the result is “pooling does not help when local history is long,” not “pooling fails.” *Permitted claim:* as in §6, with that conditioning kept visible.
1. **Within one fitted quantile model, the full predictive distribution beats its own median and implied-mean representations in 12/12 settings.** *Evidence:* single-model three-arm contrast, paired block-bootstrap CIs all excluding zero; guard G4 enforces the same fitted model behind all arms. *Importance:* the cleanest causal-within-simulation statement in the paper — representation, not estimation, is varied. *Limitation:* the value is relative to representations nobody should deliberately use inside an EV optimizer (see finding 5); and the distribution being valued is under-dispersed (77–81% empirical coverage at 90% nominal), so the measured value is of a *miscalibrated* distribution — arguably a lower bound, but say so. *Permitted claim:* “distributional information has significant positive value within the optimization policy class in this simulation.”
1. **Accuracy and simulated decision value largely agree.** *Evidence:* Spearman ρ 0.88–0.99 across all 12 city-regime cells; decision-based validation selection matches MAE-based selection in 9/12, gains in 2, never harms. *Importance:* an honest result that pushes against the paper’s own decision-aware framing — in this environment, accuracy is mostly a sufficient proxy, and decision-aware selection is a cheap safety check, not an improvement. The paper states this correctly. *Limitation:* the 2 gains both occur where MAE selection chose the pooled model — see the selection contradiction below; the count “9/12, 2 gains” is only as solid as that artifact’s account. *Permitted claim:* as in §7’s final paragraph, after the contradiction is resolved.
1. **Proportional apportionment beats the exact expected-value optimizer driven by point forecasts in all 12 settings, and beats even the full-distribution arm in 11/12.** *Evidence:* paired CIs vs. point-greedy in 12/12; descriptive 0.1–6.5% vs. the full arm. *Importance:* real, but lower than the paper’s emphasis suggests. Against the *point*-forecast optimizer the result is close to definitional: an EV optimizer fed a degenerate distribution has a piecewise-flat marginal-value profile and allocates by tie-breaking — a comparator no informed practitioner would field. The scientifically interesting part is the **11/12 edge over the full-distribution arm**, which is small, once reversed (SF/generous), and shows that in this carryover environment a robust heuristic matches or beats the per-day-optimal policy. *Limitation:* conditional on equal weights (gap compresses under normative weights — disclosed) and on the stylized dynamics. *Permitted claim:* see Claims audit — the “policy choice matters more than forecast refinement” headline must be re-anchored to the fair comparator.

## Main operational mechanism

**A credible mechanism exists, is partially demonstrated, and is stated too narrowly in print.**

The manuscript’s mechanism: “under a degenerate point-forecast distribution, capacity beyond forecast-plus-carryover has zero marginal expected value, so the exact optimizer places surplus units without information.” This is verified for the surplus regime (the budget-12/3-family example in gate finding F2). But proportional dominance holds in the **scarce** regime too, where there is little or no surplus. The mechanism there is the mirror image: under equal weights and degenerate forecasts, every unit *below* point demand-plus-carryover has identical marginal value κω across families, so the greedy ordering is equally uninformative on the deficit side — allocation is decided by tie-breaking, producing lumpy coverage that proportional apportionment avoids. The correct general statement is: **degenerate forecast distributions make the optimizer’s marginal-value profile piecewise-flat (κω below the point estimate, 0 above), so under a neutral objective the greedy ordering carries no information in either segment; distributional tails restore a gradient.** The surplus half is empirically confirmed; the deficit half is asserted by me from the model’s structure and should be confirmed with one small worked example (same cost as the existing surplus example) before the manuscript generalizes the mechanism. What is missing for the 11/12 edge over the *full-distribution* arm is any mechanism at all — the paper currently offers none, and should either identify one (plausibly: under persistent carryover, per-day-myopic optimality is not horizon optimality, and proportional happens to approximate a smoother hedging policy) or label that part of the result as an observed pattern without a demonstrated mechanism.

## Fatal or near-fatal weaknesses

**W1 — The manuscript contradicts its own selection artifact on what validation MAE chose (near-fatal as printed; resolvable without reruns).** Manuscript §6: “validation selection accordingly picks the LOCAL calendar+weather model in all four cities.” The supplement’s selection table (the artifact behind §7’s 9/12–2–1 count) shows validation MAE selecting **global**/calendar+weather in **Austin** (all three regimes) and **San Francisco** (all three regimes); §7 itself says the two Austin “gains” arise because “the decision criterion prefers the local over the pooled model” — which presupposes that MAE preferred the pooled one. The implementation report contains the same internal contradiction (§6 vs. §7). Both statements cannot describe the same selection procedure over the same candidate set. Either (a) §6’s selection excludes pooled candidates (then say so explicitly and delete “accordingly”), or (b) the two procedures consume different pooled validation predictions (e.g., different censoring stages), in which case the design must be stated and defended, or (c) one statement is stale. The owner must determine which from `validation_selection.csv` vs. `decision_selection.csv` and correct every affected sentence. A referee who notices this — and a careful one will, since both numbers are in the same PDF set — will question the integrity of the whole selection narrative. *Why near-fatal:* it touches a headline claim (pooling loses) and the selection-experiment count simultaneously.

**W2 — Scarce-regime inference rests on a trending, non-stationary loss series (near-fatal for the scarce-regime numbers as inferential statements; reframable).** The scarce regime fixes capacity at 70% of training-mean demand. With α = 0, roughly 30% of inflow accumulates as carryover every day; the simulated backlog grows approximately linearly over the ~330-day test window, and because the daily loss u_t includes the carryover stock, the daily loss series — and plausibly the paired daily *differentials* between policies — contain a deterministic trend. Three consequences. (i) The moving-block bootstrap assumes (weak) stationarity of the differential series; on a trending differential, the CI quantifies an essentially deterministic divergence, and “significance” is close to guaranteed and uninformative. Block-length insensitivity (14/28/56) does **not** diagnose this — all three lengths are blind to a common trend. (ii) The loss functional weights a request by the number of days it remains unresolved (a request unresolved for k days contributes k times to Σu). That is a defensible waiting-time-weighted objective, but the manuscript never says it, and the headline “≈72 thousand request-equivalents per day in New York under scarcity” reads as a flow when it is a stock. (iii) The regime-comparison percentages (18–23% scarce vs. 60–93% generous) are partly mechanical: in a supercritical queue, no policy can prevent linear backlog growth, so all reductions compress toward the deterministic deficit — the regime pattern is then a property of queue stability, not only of “the value of information.” None of this invalidates the *direction* of any contrast, and the moderate/generous regimes (subcritical or near-critical) are less affected. **Required:** state the loss functional’s stock/waiting-time semantics; report a trend diagnostic on the daily loss differentials per regime (a one-line addition to the inference script — descriptive, no rerun of simulations); and re-scope scarce-regime contrasts as descriptive-with-deterministic-component, or supplement them with a trend-robust summary (e.g., terminal backlog, or differentials of *served* counts, which are bounded and plausibly stationary). The 28-day block bootstrap can stay primary for the regimes where the differential series passes the diagnostic.

**W3 — The headline policy claim is anchored to the weakest comparator.** “A simple proportional rule dominates an exact expected-value optimization family” and “the choice of allocation rule can matter more than forecast refinement” derive their large margins from the point-forecast greedy arm, which the paper’s own mechanism shows to be structurally disabled. Against the fair comparator (the full-distribution arm), proportional’s edge is 0.1–6.5% with one reversal. A hostile OM reviewer will call the point-greedy arm a strawman and the headline overstated. The fix is wording, not experiments: the robust claims are (1) “feeding point forecasts into an EV optimizer is a failure mode — its flat marginal-value profile makes it lose to naive proportional apportionment everywhere,” and (2) “even against the distribution-aware optimizer, proportional apportionment is competitive to slightly better in this carryover environment.” Both are interesting; neither is “policy choice matters more than forecast refinement” tout court.

**W4 — Printed factual errors, all submission-blocking individually, all confirmed in this mirror:** (a) Table 1 caption “8 harmonized service families per city” while Chicago has seven, contradicted by the row counts in the same table (Agent 1b R1; the generator script must be fixed or a rerun re-creates it); (b) Austin dataset id `i26j-ai4z` in **both** manuscript §4 and supplement §1 — the correct id, verified against the live portal and against the package’s own `data_sources.yml` via G10, is `xwdj-i9he` (Agent 1b R3, now confirmed to extend to the supplement); (c) the city-selection criterion “full 2019–2025 coverage” in §4, false of NYC by the paper’s own account two sentences later (Agent 1b R5a); (d) supplement §1’s “the study window starts 2019-01-01” for Chicago, contradicting D14 and the manuscript. (e) The supplement’s stale title (“From Accuracy to Allocation”). These are minutes of work each, but a benchmark paper whose printed provenance strings are wrong forfeits its central virtue.

## Novelty decision

**Already known:** that accuracy and decision quality are distinct objectives (Bertsimas & Kallus; Elmachtoub & Grigas; Donti et al.); that distributional information beats point estimates in newsvendor-type allocation (classical); that public signals improve operational forecasts (Cui; Steinker); that pooling can hurt heterogeneous series; single-city 311 prediction with claimed allocation relevance (Chicago kernel work; Miami-Dade).

**Remaining new (verified to the extent possible from here, consistent with the package’s own adversarial check and my spot-check):** a multi-city 311 forecasting benchmark under one frozen protocol; the coupling of municipal-service forecasts to an explicit allocation loss with frozen pre-test environments; the controlled single-model representation contrast in a public-sector setting; the empirical accuracy-vs-decision rank-agreement evidence on public data. I could not access paywalled full-text databases; the bounded phrasing already in the manuscript (“To our knowledge no prior work…”) is the correct epistemic register and must be kept.

**Permitted wording:** “to our knowledge, the first multi-city 311 forecasting benchmark coupled to an explicit, frozen-environment allocation evaluation”; “complementary public-sector evidence” relative to DFL benchmarks.
**Prohibited wording:** “first decision-aware evaluation”; “first to show accuracy and decision value differ”; “first cross-city transfer analysis”; “first uncertainty-aware allocation”; any unbounded “first.”

## Managerial implications (evidence-supported only)

1. When candidate forecasters have similar validation error, a downstream (simulated) decision check at validation time is cheap and never harmed model choice in this benchmark — a safe addition to selection pipelines, not an established improvement.
1. Do not feed point forecasts into an expected-value capacity optimizer with a neutral objective: the resulting policy is structurally uninformed and loses even to proportional apportionment. If an optimizer is used, give it the predictive distribution.
1. Pooled/global models should not be assumed beneficial across heterogeneous municipal systems when long local history exists; temporally valid evaluation is essential before adopting them, and contaminated validation can manufacture spurious pooling advantages (the R1-F1 episode is itself an implication).
1. Operational evaluation should be conducted under the intended resource regime, with the caveat that under structural under-capacity, differences between forecasting systems compress (this paper, conditional on the simulation).
1. Weather value in demand forecasting is system-specific and conditional on day-ahead forecast availability; validate per system rather than importing it as a stylized fact.

Prohibited implication types (none currently claimed; keep it so): staffing recommendations, deployment readiness, city-specific magnitudes, fairness improvement, causal service improvement.
