\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Explicit simulation boundary.} The allocation layer is a
transparent simulation over abstract request-equivalent capacity units;
it omits shifts, travel, specialization, geography, and intra-day
dynamics and estimates no city's actual capacity, productivity, or
queues. Conclusions concern the accuracy-to-allocation mapping inside
this simulated environment.
\item \textbf{Queue instability and horizon dependence.} Under the scarce
(and to a lesser degree moderate) regimes the simulated system is
structurally under-capacitated, so the unresolved stock grows over the
horizon; cumulative-loss magnitudes and reductions depend on horizon
length, regime comparisons are partly mechanical, and decision-contrast
intervals are descriptive rather than stationary inference
(Section~\ref{sec:robust}).
\item \textbf{Objective dependence.} The policy-ordering result is
conditional on the equal request-equivalent-weights objective; the
normative-weight sensitivity compresses the gap. Equal weights are a
deliberately simple choice, not a normatively neutral one, and no
weighting here is socially validated.
\item \textbf{Reported demand only.} Targets and losses are functions of
reported requests; unreported need is invisible to the forecasters and
the simulated planner (Section~\ref{sec:responsible}).
\item \textbf{Harmonization is a modeling choice.} The service families
are deterministic keyword buckets; native taxonomies differ across cities
(Chicago lacks a residential-noise intake type; excluding Austin's large
residual \emph{other} leaves conclusions unchanged). All mappings and
compositions are published.
\item \textbf{Weather information set.} Target-day weather uses realized
observations as an oracle proxy for an accurate day-ahead forecast (A3);
lagged weather is a lower-information comparison, not a substitute for
evaluating archived day-ahead forecasts with realistic forecast error.
One station per city coarsens spatial variation (A2); wind was excluded
for data completeness.
\item \textbf{Calibration.} The quantile intervals are under-dispersed
(90\% nominal, 77--81\% empirical); split-conformal recalibration
(restoring 88--90\% coverage) leaves the allocation result essentially
unchanged (Sections~\ref{sec:forecast}--\ref{sec:decision}), so the
decision finding rests on identification of the objective, not on
calibrated uncertainty.
\item \textbf{Scope.} Four U.S. Socrata-based systems; fixed a priori
hyperparameters; no deep or foundation models; no causal claims; no
deployment; no measurement of social need.
\end{itemize}
